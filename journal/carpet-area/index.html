Carpet area, plainly

Why every area on this site is a carpet area, what the law counts, and what it leaves out.

For years, Indian homes were sold by "super built-up" area: the flat plus a share of the corridors, lifts, walls and clubhouse, inflated by a loading factor buyers often could not check. The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 changed the unit of sale to carpet area.

What the law counts

"Carpet area" means the net usable floor area of an apartment, excluding the area covered by external walls, areas under services shafts, exclusive balcony or verandah area and exclusive open terrace area, but includes the area covered by the internal partition walls of the apartment.

Section 2(k), Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, as reproduced by TaxGuru, with two typing errors in that reproduction corrected. Check the official text before relying on it.

So the inside of your home counts, including the walls between rooms. Its outer walls, shafts, and your own balconies, verandahs and terraces don't. Those are real space and often valuable, but they are listed separately.

How this site uses it

Every area on Rainfold's pages is a carpet area. The 41 m² verandah, the garden-level gardens and the roof terraces appear as "open space", on their own line. There is no super built-up figure anywhere, because it describes nothing you can stand in.

An illustrative example

A middle-level valley home: 1,720 sq ft carpet, plus a 440 sq ft verandah listed separately. Not a price, not an offer, and not an availability statement.
