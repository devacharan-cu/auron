Water held, not drained

The valley floor, the city’s old tanks, and a garden that is a reservoir for four months a year.

Bengaluru grew up around tanks. Over centuries, hundreds of them, kere in Kannada, were built across the plateau and linked by channels so that one overflowed into the next down each valley. Many have been built over or polluted since, and the city now floods in the same valleys it once used to store water.

A fold is a drain, or a reservoir

A shallow valley like the one Rainfold imagines carries water whatever you build on it. The usual approach to land like this is to level it into platforms and pipe the run-off away. Rainfold does the opposite. Its roofs and slopes drain to the valley floor, where a chain of shallow pools slows the water down and lets it soak in. What the pools cannot hold overflows north, as the old channels did, toward a tank.

Four wet months, eight dry ones

Most of Bengaluru's rain falls between June and October. For those months the rain garden is water, reeds and the sound of both. For the rest of the year it is a garden of stones and grasses, and the walk beside it is the shadiest place on the site. Neither state is the "real" one. The design is for both.

The pools, planting and drainage here are illustrative. A real scheme would need a hydrological study and the agreement of the local authority.
