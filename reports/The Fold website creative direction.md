# Unfold the land, then sell the plan

The best direction for The Fold is an architect's drawing set that unfolds into a landscape. It should not be a cinematic 3D showpiece. Build it around one master drawing, a section through the folded landform that the building really is, and let the site open chapter by chapter, from land to form, light, homes, proof and visit. The evidence across five research streams points the same way. Award-winning residential sites score lowest on usability. Buyers rate floor plans well above virtual tours (57% vs 41% "very useful"). No independent study shows that interactive 3D raises enquiries on a developer website. And because The Fold is fictional, a photoreal 3D model would imply facts that do not exist. The opening is elsewhere. Most Indian premium developer sites are opaque corporate portfolios: generic "iconic luxury" copy, no floor plans or carpet areas on project pages, RERA details pushed to the edges. Architecture studios keep their most convincing material (sections, models, material studies) in archives instead of on their homepages. Form & Field can win by putting that material up front and pairing it with more transparency than local competitors offer: carpet area first, honest infrastructure timelines, a construction log and a compliance block styled as calmly as the rest of the page. Interactivity belongs where it answers a buyer's question: a light study scrubbed by time of day, an SVG residence locator with a text twin, and plan cards bound to facing and view. At most one line-drawn WebGL massing model, loaded only when the visitor asks for it. One caveat applies throughout: the researchers read page source and copy and did not view the sites. Every claim here about photography, colour and motion is inferred and needs a browser walk-through before art direction is locked.

## Award galleries reward spectacle, but buyers reward plans

Few residential sites win awards, and the ones that do are weak on usability. ERA Residence in Estepona was Awwwards Site of the Day on 31 August 2026 with **7.61 overall, Design 7.81 and Usability 7.3** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/era-residence)). Silver Pinewood, also Site of the Day, scored **7.25 with Usability lowest at 7.02** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/silver-pinewood-residences)). Mumbai's 25 Residences, the closest Indian luxury peer at award level, scored **Animations 7.80 but Usability 7.06** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/25-residences)). Meanwhile the most commercially complete sites in the sample, The Perigon and Una, won nothing. The lesson for Form & Field is that award galleries are mood boards, not UX benchmarks.

The global set also shows how commoditised the premium formula has become. Seven of the twelve sites whose fonts could be identified use a serif plus grotesk pairing drawn from a small group of foundries; Lyon appears on both Aman and Una. Barba page transitions run on at least six sites, Lenis smooth scroll on two, and hero film is standard. "Residential masterpiece", "Manhattan's Newest Landmark", "quiet luxury" and "This is forever" all appear live ([FontMirror](https://www.fontmirror.com/en/how-luxury-real-estate-websites-sell-a-lifestyle/); [Perigon](https://theperigonmiamibeach.com/); [111w57](https://111w57.com/); [Silver Pinewood](https://silver-pinewood.com/); [One Circular Quay](https://www.onecircularquay.com/)). None of this sets a site apart any more. What still does is a structural idea tied to the project, an uncommon display face, and honest specifics.

ERA's rows ("№ 011, Block B1, 0 floor, 3 bed / 132 m² + 29 m² Terrace, Completion 4Q 2026") show that a typographic, monochrome list can feel premium. No live 3D stacking configurator turned up anywhere in the global sample. Note that the immersive-experience research describes a separate Vide Infra "ERA" build for developer Tekta: a Three.js district map and a parametric 3D selector ([Vide Infra](https://videinfra.com/blog/case-study-a-triple-site-of-the-day-winner-powered-by-webgl)). The notes do not establish whether that is the same project as era-residence.com, so treat the two as separate references until you have checked.

## Studios hide their best material, which leaves an opening

Architecture practices hold exactly the material that would make a developer credible, and they rarely put it on the homepage. Herzog & de Meuron's drawings, models, "material experiments and full-scale mockups" live in the Kabinett archive, which is not open to the public ([H&dM Kabinett](https://www.herzogdemeuron.com/kabinett/)). Studio Mumbai's homepage holds nothing but a name, a map and an address ([studiomumbai.com](https://www.studiomumbai.com)). Its process is documented elsewhere: 335 drawings and 67 models at the CCA, including **"tape drawings" made on plywood during the monsoon "when paper was too humid to use on site"** ([CCA](https://www.cca.qc.ca/en/search/details/library/publication/953394589)). Snøhetta is the exception that proves the point: "Process" is one of only three top-level nav items ([snohetta.com](https://www.snohetta.com)). A developer that leads with sections, site models, material samples and a dated construction diary would stand out against the usual render carousel without imitating any single studio.

Several studio conventions transfer directly. **Separate credits for renders and photographs**, as H&dM ("Visualization: RENWERK") and ZHA ("Render by Devisual") use them, build trust at no cost ([herzogdemeuron.com](https://www.herzogdemeuron.com); [zha.com](https://www.zha.com/?disclaimer=true)). For a fictional project this honesty becomes a feature: every image labelled "Visualisation", "Model, 1:200" or "Material sample". Studio Lotus tags each project with live status lines such as "Construction in full-swing" ([studiolotus.in](https://www.studiolotus.in)), which is effectively a construction-progress signal. West 8 heads each project card with an editorial line before the project name ("A Living Museum of Plants") and groups its thinking under an "Approach" taxonomy ([west8.com](https://www.west8.com)). That suits the "Field" half of the brand. Catalogue numbering feels premium at H&dM because more than 600 projects stand behind it. A single-project developer should therefore number the parts of The Fold (residences F-01 to F-06, sheets in a drawing set, chapters 01 to 07), not invent a portfolio.

What not to copy matters just as much. Studio Mumbai's contact-only homepage, Oudolf's caption-free thumbnails and OMA's icon-only, client-rendered nav (which gave a crawler almost nothing) all work for practices whose clients already know them. A sales site that withholds information loses leads ([oma.com](https://www.oma.com); [oudolf.com](https://oudolf.com)). The trendy studio kit (Inter throughout, zero-radius cards, monospace "technical" annotations, grey index tables) is now standard in template marketplaces ([v0 template](https://0jo2xuhypsg.v0.build/templates/YqU0WZeWjDF)), so on its own it reads as a 2025 portfolio theme rather than an institution. Studio signatures such as BIG's per-project icons, ZHA's parametric swooshes and Studio Mumbai's workshop imagery are off-limits.

The strongest precedent for selling through the architecture is a developer brand: **Pentagram's 11 Hoyt** for Studio Gang's Brooklyn tower. The wordmark echoes the building's slender verticality, the palette is drawn from the interiors, the sales gallery had a projection-mapped neighbourhood model, and the sales kit was a linen clamshell box, yet the website was kept "light and easy for prospective buyers to navigate" ([Pentagram](https://pentagram.com/work/11-hoyt/story)). Pentagram's architecture identities follow the same rule: take one real detail and keep the system restrained. Hyphen built a stencil alphabet from the crossbar of its "H" and uses a mostly monochrome palette with a single accent ([Pentagram, Hyphen](https://www.pentagram.com/work/hyphen)). St Paul's wordmark came from the cathedral's own details ([casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/pentagram/case-study/st-pauls-cathedral-creates-a-flexible-new-brand-identity-with-pentagram)).

## North Bengaluru gives The Fold a believable shape and a transparency gap

For a fictional project to feel real, its typology, price and location have to match the market. Bengaluru was the **fastest-growing major Indian housing market in H1 2026, with 27,968 sales (up 5%) at an average ₹9,354/sq ft** ([Knight Frank](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/3116/documents/en/india-real-estate-office-and-residential-market-h1-2026-12927.pdf)). **Hebbal led the city's ₹10 Cr+ segment with 22% of sales value** in FY25 ([The Realty Today](https://therealtytoday.com/news/bengalurus-ultra-luxury-housing-market-crosses-1000-crore-sales-milestone-in-fy25)). Commentators also note "a visible shift toward villa communities and low density housing" ([Trade Brains](https://tradebrains.in/money/top-4-indian-cities-where-luxury-housing-dominates-the-realty-market-in-2026/)). The most believable form for something called The Fold is a **low-rise, landform-driven community in the Yelahanka–Jakkur–Bagalur belt**: courtyard and terrace residences under folded, planted roofs, with distant sightlines to Nandi Hills. Total Environment's After the Rain shows that earth-sheltered, courtyard villas sell in Yelahanka ([JLL Homes](https://www.jllhomes.co.in/villas-new/bengaluru/total-environment-after-the-rain-4-bhk-villas-yelahanka-331000-sqft)). That precedent is a warning as well as validation: The Fold's architecture and naming must not read as a copy of it. A plausible fictional brief, triangulated from broker and developer sources whose figures conflict, is 3,000–5,500 sq ft homes at roughly ₹10,000–14,000/sq ft (about ₹4–8 Cr) for low-rise in this belt ([PropNewz](https://www.propnewz.com/blog/villa-hotspots-north-bangalore-devanahalli-yelahanka-hebbal-2026); [Sobha blog](https://www.sobha.com/blog/flats-in-north-bangalore-vs-east-bangalore/)). Present it as "from ₹X Cr, all-inclusive pricing on request", not as exact figures.

Infrastructure copy is where honesty is easiest to show. The Blue Line to the airport was **72% complete in August 2026, with completion targeted for end-2027** ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/tired-of-bengaluru-traffic-airport-metro-crosses-72-mark-heres-what-it-means-for-you-547584-2026-08-06)), and other reports put the full line at March 2028 ([Native Planet](https://www.nativeplanet.com/news/bengaluru-metros-blue-line-namma-metros-blue-line-services-will-be-operational-in-march-2028-023833.html)). The remaining STRR stretch has slipped to November 2026 ([Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/news-brief/144-km-of-bengaluru-satellite-town-ring-road-set-to-open-by-november-2026-report)). Lodha's STRR explainer still showed a December 2025 deadline ([Lodha](https://www.lodhagroup.com/blogs/infrastructure-and-connectivity/bangalore-satellite-town-ring-road-map-status)). A map that says "expected 2027, dated source" will read as more premium than "minutes to the airport".

Indian premium developer sites leave plenty of room. Embassy's homepage leads with "Exceeding Expectations" and "YOUR SIGNATURE LUXURY". Its Lake Terraces project page has **no floor plans, carpet areas, price or specifications**, although it does show the RERA number and status directly under the overview ([embassyindia.com](https://embassyindia.com/); [Embassy Lake Terraces](https://embassyindia.com/project/embassy-lake-terraces/)). The Fold should keep that RERA placement and go beyond the rest. Isprava qualifies leads without publishing prices by asking for a **budget band (below 7 Cr / 7–10 Cr / 10–15 Cr / 15 Cr+)** in a multi-step enquiry ([isprava.com](https://www.isprava.com/)). No Indian developer site recognised by Awwwards was confirmed in this research, although 25 Residences shows that Indian luxury residential can win Site of the Day.

Compliance shapes the design. Karnataka RERA guidance requires the registration number "on the top right corner" of visual ads, in a font no smaller than the project name's, a reference to the K-RERA website, all-inclusive pricing, and **no "subject to change" disclaimers** ([Corpbiz](https://corpbiz.io/learning/guidelines-related-to-rera-karnataka/); [Dwello](https://dwello.in/news/karnataka-rera-rules-on-advertising-and-marketing-in-real-estate)). These come from secondary summaries; the primary circular was not located. For a fictional brand the ethical rule outranks the regulatory one. Never use the real `PRM/KA/RERA/1251/309/PR/...` number format. Use a placeholder that is plainly fictional ("K-RERA: CONCEPT ONLY · NOT A REGISTERED PROJECT"). Use non-routable phone numbers and example.com addresses, dummy lead forms that say they are demos, no fabricated testimonials, awards or press, and a persistent "fictional, for a design portfolio, not an offer" disclaimer. Trademark availability for "Form & Field" and "The Fold" in India was not checked.

## Four creative directions, tested against the brief

The research supports at least four distinct directions. Each was tested for originality, fit with the name, believability for a fictional project, buyer usefulness and build risk.

| Direction | Idea | Strengths | Weaknesses | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. The Drawing Set** | The site as an architect's document: numbered sheets, sections, plans, line drawings drawn on scroll, title blocks as UI | Original among Indian developers; honest about being a concept; light to build in SVG; credible "Form" voice | Can feel cold and technical; risks drifting into the monospace and Swiss-grid template look; drawings alone do not convey living | Strong as the system, too dry as the whole |
| **B. Land, Unfolding** | Landscape-led: topography, contour lines, native planting, Deccan boulders, twin monsoons, seasons. Building and land read as one folded surface | Fits "Field" and "Fold" literally; draws on Bengaluru's garden-city and lake (kere) culture; warm and sensory | Needs strong landscape imagery the project does not have; "nature" copy slides into "lush greenery" cliché; close to After the Rain's earth-sheltered story | Strong as the emotional layer, and must be grounded in real species and materials |
| **C. A Day of Light** | Time as the organising device: dawn to night, dry season to monsoon. Day/night and seasonal toggles; light studies per residence | Turns "climate-responsive" from adjective into evidence, which no audited Indian studio site does; maps to a real buyer question (sun and facing); ERA's day/night toggle proves the device | Implies precise solar claims for a building that does not exist; as a full structure it can become gimmicky | Use as the signature interaction, labelled "illustrative" |
| **D. The Monolith** | Cinematic WebGL: a 3D model as hero, orbiting camera, shader transitions, reflective landscape, as in the reported Hubtown "3D monolith" | Memorable; award-bait; showcases technical craft | Usability is consistently the weakest score on such sites; heavy on mid-range Android and 4G; a photoreal fictional model implies false facts; the "monolith" is the opposite of a fold | Reject as the primary direction |
| *E. The Hospitality Magazine* (also considered) | Aman-style editorial lifestyle, resort tone | Polished, familiar to HNI buyers | Overpromises without hotel operations; generic; the "quiet luxury" cliché | Reject |

Direction D deserves a fair hearing, because it is what clients tend to ask for. Its case is real: 25 Residences shows that an Indian luxury site built on animation and 3D can win Site of the Day ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/25-residences)), and portal data links 3D tours with **32% more saves and 29% more views** on Zillow ([We Get Around, citing Zillow](https://forum.we-get-around.com/topic/14259/zillow-stats-buyers-saved-listings-with-3d-home-tours-32-more-often-/)). But those figures are self-reported, correlational and about captured tours of real, resale homes, not off-plan models. That is why the recommendation combines A, B and C and keeps D at the margins: **the drawing set as the system, the land as the feeling, and light as the interaction.**

## Does 3D earn its place? Only as a locator, never as the show

On buyer value, the evidence comes down against heavy 3D. In NAR's 2025 Generational Trends (via secondary summaries that disagree slightly), buyers rated photos "very useful" at 83%, detailed property information at 79%, **floor plans at 57% and virtual tours at 41%** ([nowbam](https://nowbam.com/88-of-home-buyers-still-rely-on-agents-nar-2025-report-finds/)). In Zillow's 2021 survey, **74% said a dynamic floor plan helps them decide** whether a home is right for them ([homediary](https://homediary.com/pro/?p=54938)). No independent A/B or conversion study of an interactive 3D developer site was found. The award-winning WebGL case study reports awards, not leads. The headline "10x revenue" claim for PropVR and DAMAC is vendor marketing whose baseline could not be checked ([Unreal Engine](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/spotlights/propvrs-digital-twins-help-real-estate-developer-boost-revenue-by-10x)). Research on parallax, the closest proxy for scroll-driven 3D, found no significant usability gain and recorded **two participants with motion sickness** ([UXPA Journal](https://uxpajournal.org/?p=2505); [Purdue](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cgttheses/27/)).

The costs fall hardest on this project. ERA's WebGL team found a solid model "too heavy" and switched to **line-based rendering with vertex-only files** to save weight ([Vide Infra](https://videinfra.com/blog/case-study-a-triple-site-of-the-day-winner-powered-by-webgl)). India's mobile median looks high, but **4G median download was about 18.93 Mbps** in Q4 2023 ([Statista citing Ookla](https://statista.com/statistics/1496635/india-median-5g-and-4g-performance)), and that is what a buyer on congested peri-urban coverage gets. Capture techniques (Gaussian splats, Matterport, Zillow's SkyTour) need a real built space ([LiDAR News](https://lidarnews.com/zillow-3d-tours-with-gaussian-splatting/)), so they are ruled out. Geo-accurate sun simulation of the Shadowmap kind depends on real surrounding geometry ([Shadowmap](https://shadowmap.org/blog/shed-light-on-your-real-estate-offerings)), so it would be false precision here. The deepest risk is credibility. A low-fidelity model reads as fake, and a high-fidelity one reads as a promise.

The verdict is that 3D improves The Fold only in one narrow form: a **stylised, line-drawn or flat-shaded massing model that orients the site and highlights a selected residence**. It runs from the same data as an SVG locator, loads only when the visitor taps "Explore the site in 3D" on a capable device, uses a constrained camera, and has no lens effects. The line-drawn look is honest about being a concept, matches the drawing-set system, and is the lightest option technically. Build version one without it. Prove the SVG locator first, then add the WebGL layer if a device-tier prototype shows it adds orientation that the isometric SVG cannot.

## Recommended direction: "The Folded Section"

### Creative direction and visual concept

The concept is a single section drawing through The Fold, showing ground, folded planted roof, courtyard and sky. It works as logo seed, homepage spine, chapter divider and interactive canvas. The site opens on that line, drawn in as the page loads, then lifts into the first photograph. Each chapter is a "sheet" in the set (01 Land, 02 Form, 03 Light, 04 Residences, 05 Field, 06 Proof, 07 Visit), the 200 East 83rd chapter structure adapted to a landform rather than a tower. The emotional register comes from the land: rain on granite, rain-tree canopy, the northern horizon towards Nandi Hills, and the first-rain smell named in copy rather than shown as stock. The register for information is the drawing set: dimensioned plans, labelled sheets, dated logs. Emotion leads and logic follows, the sequence the best sites share ([FontMirror](https://www.fontmirror.com/en/how-luxury-real-estate-websites-sell-a-lifestyle/)). The architecture must genuinely be folded in every render and drawing. A folded-plane logo on a conventional building would be exposed as decoration.

### Brand identity

The masterbrand and the project should speak in two related voices. **Form & Field** is the restrained, institutional voice, in the spirit of Chipperfield and H&dM. **The Fold** is warmer and more sensory, like West 8's editorial headlines. The Form & Field wordmark can turn the **ampersand into the hinge** where built form meets open field, echoing the way the ampersand sits inside H&dM's short form ([herzogdemeuron.com](https://www.herzogdemeuron.com)). Set "Form" in a more constructed letterform and "Field" in a softer one, so the duality is expressed by the type rather than stated. Check the "F&F" shorthand against the existing Indian fashion label first. The Fold's mark is a single crease taken from the real section: one line, one change of direction. The secondary graphic system pairs an architectural grid (form) with topographic contours (field), and carries across hoardings, site signage, the brochure folio and web dividers.

For colour, swatch real materials, following 11 Hoyt's interior-derived palette, not the black-and-gold luxury default: laterite or red-earth ochre, granite grey, lime-plaster off-white, rain-tree green and monsoon slate. Run mostly monochrome (lime and granite) with one accent, as Hyphen does ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/hyphen)). For type, use at most two families. Pair one characterful display serif, ideally customised or uncommon, with a disciplined humanist sans that also has good Kannada-script support or a matched Kannada companion for place names and wayfinding touches. Avoid Bodoni or Didone with Helvetica, Lyon-style serif plus grotesk with no idea behind it, Inter throughout, and monospace "technical" labels. Specific typeface choices were not researched, so they are open for the art director.

### Photography and imagery

Because nothing is built, the imagery has to be honest about its source, and that honesty can become a style. Use four labelled image classes. **Visualisations** are calm, overcast or low-sun renders with real Bengaluru planting, credited as visualisation. **Model photography** shows a physical or digital 1:200 model shot like an object, the 11 Hoyt sales-gallery instinct. **Material close-ups** cover granite, lime plaster, terracotta jaali, Mangalore tile and timber. **Land photography** shows the North Bengaluru plateau, boulders and lakes, used as context, not as a claim to a specific parcel. Attach a per-image "artist's impression" label, as One Circular Quay does ([One Circular Quay](https://www.onecircularquay.com/residences/full-floor/)). Avoid drone fly-throughs of CGI at sunset, champagne-by-the-pool shots, stock families and amenity collages. People, where they appear, should be Bengaluru residents in ordinary moments: multigenerational families, a founder working in a courtyard.

### Signature interactions

Seven interactions carry the experience. Each answers a buyer question.

| Interaction | Buyer question | Build | Accessible twin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The section draws itself**: the master section line draws on load and on chapter entry, then "folds" into the next sheet | What is this place? (brand) | SVG plus GSAP DrawSVG; poster state under reduced motion | Static drawing with caption |
| **Light study**: a time-of-day and season slider over a residence plan showing sun arc and shadow wedge, with dry season, monsoon and night states | Will my living room get morning sun? Is it hot in the afternoon? | Canvas 2D or SVG; `<input type="range">`; labelled "illustrative" | Text summary per residence ("Courtyard receives direct morning sun, December") |
| **Residence locator**: an isometric SVG of the site; tap a cluster, then a home, to highlight position, facing arrow and status | Where is my home, and what does it face? | SVG with roving tabindex; same JSON as the list | Filterable list/table (cluster, level, configuration, facing, status) |
| **Plan card**: plan with carpet area first, super built-up second, sq ft/sq m toggle, dimensions on tap, furniture toggle, compass, entrance direction | Does the layout work? Is it vastu-friendly? | SVG; container queries for panel and full-width | HTML room-and-area table |
| **Optional 3D site model**: a line-drawn massing, constrained orbit, highlights the selected home | How does it all sit together? | three.js on WebGL2, intent-loaded, GPU-gated | Locator and list (3D is never the only route) |
| **Honest map**: a stylised North Bengaluru map with travel-time rings and dated infrastructure ("Blue Line, Hebbal–Airport, expected 2027") | How connected is it, really? | SVG | Text list of destinations and sources |
| **Making The Fold log**: dated construction entries, drawings, material library, collaborators | Will it be delivered, and is it well made? | Static pages from a JSON/Markdown journal | Native HTML |

The conversion layer stays quiet but always reachable. A persistent "Book a visit" sits next to a WhatsApp "Request a viewing" (pointing to a demo, non-routable target). The short enquiry form has a buyer-type toggle (end-user / investor / NRI / channel partner), an Isprava-style budget band, a preferred callback time, and a promised reply time. All of it is clearly marked as a portfolio demo.

### Technical approach

Build a **Vite multi-page site with vanilla ES modules and Tailwind v4**. Design tokens (palette, type scale, spacing) live in `@theme`, so the same CSS variables drive Tailwind utilities, SVG fills, Canvas drawing and shader uniforms. Tailwind v4 needs Chrome 111, Safari 16.4 and Firefox 128 ([Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/compatibility)); check traffic from older Android WebViews. Each interactive feature is a `data-feature` element whose module loads through dynamic `import()` when it nears the viewport (IntersectionObserver), so three.js never sits in the initial bundle. Use **GSAP ScrollTrigger and DrawSVG**, now free for commercial use under a licence that is not open source ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free); [GSAP licence](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/)), for the section-drawing choreography. Add CSS `animation-timeline: view()` only as decorative progressive enhancement: it reaches **87.84% global usage, but Firefox stable still lacks it** ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline)). Cross-document View Transitions can morph a residence card into its detail page in Chrome and Safari 18.2+, and fall back to a normal navigation where unsupported, including **Samsung Internet, which has no support** ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions)). Keep native scroll; Lenis is optional on desktop only and respects reduced motion by default ([Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)).

The optional 3D layer uses three.js on WebGL2, not a WebGPU dependency. The geometry is a low-poly or line model compressed with glTF-Transform (Meshopt, KTX2) and targets 1.5–3 MB or less on mobile ([gltf-transform](https://gltf-transform.dev/cli)). It is gated by self-hosted `detect-gpu`, whose benchmark data stopped updating in December 2025 ([detect-gpu](https://github.com/pmndrs/detect-gpu)), plus `saveData` and reduced-motion checks. Cap device pixel ratio around 1.5 and render on demand. Performance targets are **LCP ≤ 2.5s, INP ≤ 200ms and CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile on mobile** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)), met with an HTML/CSS hero, AVIF images (96% support, [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/avif)) and no WebGL on the critical path. A working budget is roughly 150–200 KB of gzipped JS before interaction and a hero image of 100 KB or less. These are starting points, not standards. Accessibility follows WCAG 2.2: single-pointer alternatives for any drag or orbit (2.5.7), **24×24 px minimum targets** for rooms and hotspots (2.5.8), and sticky headers that never hide focus (2.4.11) ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). Availability is never shown by colour alone, and every canvas has a DOM twin.

## Conclusion

The research turns the usual brief on its head. The scarce asset in luxury property web design is no longer polish, motion or 3D, since every award-level site has those and scores lowest on usability. It is **verifiable specificity**: drawings, dated logs, carpet areas, light that a visitor can test, and timelines with sources. For a fictional developer that is doubly true, because honesty is the only credibility it can claim. Labels such as "Visualisation", "illustrative" and "concept only" stop being legal hedges and become part of the art direction.

Some open questions remain before build. The art director needs to walk through the shortlisted sites in a browser, since no visual inspection was done. Typefaces, the trademark check and the primary K-RERA circular are still unverified. The physical form of The Fold must be designed so that the folded section is true, not decorative. And a quick mid-range Android prototype should settle whether the line-drawn 3D model earns a place in version two. If those hold, Form & Field can claim a position no Bengaluru developer currently occupies: the architect's transparency, shown with a landscape designer's warmth.
