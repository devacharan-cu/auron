# Form & Field / The Fold — Refined Creative Direction

## Context
The first research round (`reports/The Fold website creative direction.md`) recommended "The Folded Section": a section through ground, a **folded planted roof** and a courtyard, in a palette of laterite, terracotta jaali, granite and lime. This stage tests that against real references in a live browser, verifies the open legal and naming questions, and settles a direction strong enough to design from. No project files or code are created.

**What was actually inspected (Claude in Chrome, desktop 1440–1477 px, 11 Oct 2026):**
- **After the Rain:** the Total Environment project page, which partly froze, plus an image survey.
- **The Fold, Dubai:** an image survey only.
- **Other sites:** 200east83rd.com, era-residence.com (home and /apartments), theperigonmiamibeach.com/residences, 25residences.com, chelseabarracks.com, Embassy Lake Terraces, herzogdemeuron.com and Pentagram's 11 Hoyt page.
- **Not inspected:**
  - west8.com was blocked by the extension's site permissions.
  - Milieu, Neometro, The OWO and Snøhetta were not opened this pass.
  - Mobile views failed because the window would not resize below desktop, so **no responsive inspection was achieved**.

## 1. Verdict on The Folded Section, and the final concept

**The original version must change. A folded planted roof is almost exactly After the Rain.**
- **After the Rain, observed:** low duplex villas, each with a turf roof that slopes down into the ground. Exposed red wire-cut brick with corbelled brick jaali bands. Warm timber interiors at dusk.
- **Overlap with our draft:** "ground + folded planted roof", plus laterite, terracotta jaali and an earth-and-rain story, would read as a copy.
- **A second collision:** **"The Fold" (Dubai, by Tkdp)** is a widely published, award-winning townhouse project. Its look is white, curved, tulip-like fins with timber louvres. Sculptural folded façades are now taken too.

**Final concept: the fold is in the ground, not on the building. Working name: "The Folded Section, re-cut."**
- **The site:** The Fold sits in a shallow fold of the North Bengaluru plateau, a valley-like dip of the kind that carries monsoon run-off toward a tank (*kere*).
- **The architecture:** pale, horizontal, terraced mid-rise bands of lime plaster and granite that step down both flanks.
- **The valley floor:** a shared landscape of shade trees, boulders and a rain garden.
- **One section drawing:**
  - The ground line dips, buildings sit on both slopes, the sun's arc passes overhead and a water line sits at the bottom.
  - It is the logo seed (one dipping line), the homepage spine, the residence locator's second view and the light-study surface.
  - The fold is real topography, so the drawing is the truth of the project, not decoration.
- **Why this beats the alternatives:**
  - It differs from After the Rain (low brick, sloped sod roofs) and from Dubai's The Fold (sculptural white fins).
  - It is honest for a fictional project, because it is about landform rather than a specific parcel.
  - It gives every drawing a reason to exist.

**Alternatives weighed and rejected as the lead:**
- *Pure drawing set:* kept as the system, but too dry to lead.
- *"The Ledger":* a radical-transparency, data-first developer. Its voice is kept for the Record section, but it is cold as a whole identity.
- *Cinematic WebGL:* not justified by the evidence. 25 Residences and 200 E 83rd show the cost: long preloaders and blank screens.
- *Hotel lifestyle:* ERA's staged CGI people read as a resort.

**Brand identity:**
- **Form & Field:** institutional and restrained. The wordmark's ampersand is the hinge (from the first report), set on a baseline that subtly dips, borrowed from the section.
- **The Fold:** its mark is the single dipping section line, nothing more.
- **Graphic system:** an orthogonal drawing grid (form) crossed by contour lines (field).
- **Naming:** Total Environment's naming signature is song and book titles ("In That Quiet Earth", "The Meadow Dance", "Down by the Water"). Never name residence types or places that way. Use the drawing-set numbering instead (F-01…, Sheet 02).

## 3. Visual language

- **Colour has meaning, not just mood.**
  - Lime-wash paper (warm off-white) as the base, with charcoal ink (kadappa-stone black) for all linework.
  - Granite grey as the secondary tone and deep canopy green for landscape.
  - **One accent, sun ochre, used only where light or sun data is shown**, so the colour itself signals "this is the light study".
  - No brick red or terracotta lead (that belongs to After the Rain), and no black and gold.
- **Typography:**
  - What I saw: high-contrast Didone or condensed serifs on ERA, 200E83rd and 25 Residences; Haas Unica neo-grotesk on Total Environment. Both are now category defaults.
  - Recommendation: one humanist sans as the workhorse, used for facts, annotations and dimension-style numerals.
  - Add one low-contrast text serif only for long-form narrative, with a matched Kannada face (for example Noto Sans/Serif Kannada) for place names.
  - Specific families still need trial and licence checks.
- **Composition:**
  - Every page is a "sheet" with a slim title block (sheet number, title, scale, status), used as real UI: breadcrumb, status and disclaimer live there.
  - Large numerals set like dimension callouts ("3 BHK / 2,140 sq ft carpet").
  - Photographs sit inside the drawing grid rather than as full-bleed wallpaper, except one full-bleed land image per page.
- **Imagery:**
  - Four labelled classes from report 1: visualisation, model, material, land.
  - Material subjects: granite boulders and outcrops, lime plaster, shade, water in a channel, rain-tree canopy.
  - Ban brick-jaali close-ups, sod roofs, sunset CGI flythroughs and staged CGI people.
- **Motion:**
  - Content is visible at first paint: no preloader and no reveal-gated content.
  - Motion means only two things: a line completing, and light moving.
  - Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and give it a visible toggle.

## 4. Homepage narrative and site architecture

**Evaluation of Land → Form → Light → Residences → Field → Proof → Visit:**
- Seven chapters is too long.
- Land and Field duplicate each other.
- Light separated from Form hides the main point, which is how the section shapes the light.
- Proof comes too late for Indian buyers, who distrust under-construction claims.

**Proposed: five sheets, with Visit always available rather than a chapter.**
- **00 The Fold:** the section line, one sentence, and a status title block: "Concept project · all content illustrative".
- **01 Ground:** the plateau, the fold, planting and water, and an honest note on location (merges Land and Field).
- **02 Section:** how the homes step along the fold. The **living section** (§5) lives here, so Form and Light become one sheet.
- **03 Homes:** typology cards with carpet area first, and an entry point to the locator.
- **04 Record:** status, drawings, a dated log, the RERA placeholder and disclosures. Proof moves up from 6th to 5th of 5 and sits right after the homes.

The logic runs place → idea → home → trust → act.

**Pages:**
- **Nav:** The Fold · Residences · Location · Record · Visit, with a persistent Visit / WhatsApp (demo) button.
- **Pages beyond the nav:**
  - residence detail pages (F-01…)
  - Form & Field (developer, principles, buyer guides)
  - Disclosures (fictional statement, image labelling policy, demo-form note)

## 5. Signature interactions to prototype first (ranked)

1. **The living section.**
   - The homepage section drawing, with a time-of-day control (scrubbed on scroll and also available as an `<input type=range>`).
   - It moves the sun across the arc and casts 2D light and shadow onto the slopes and into the homes.
   - This **merges three earlier ideas** (section draws itself, light study, day/night) into one interaction.
   - The solar geometry can be honest even though the building is fictional. At about 13° N the midsummer sun passes **north of overhead** (May–July), so north-facing rooms get summer sun. That is a real, little-known Bengaluru insight; label the building geometry "illustrative".
2. **Two-view residence locator.**
   - Site plan along the fold plus section/stack, with the selected home highlighted in both, following the Perigon pattern.
   - A filterable list (configuration, level, facing, status) is the same data, and stays as the accessible twin.
   - **This replaces the isometric SVG locator**, which is costly to draw, cramped to tap on phones, and not a view architects use.
3. **Plan card.**
   - ERA-style hierarchy: № / level / facing, then "3 BHK / carpet sq ft" (sq ft/m² toggle), then super built-up second.
   - Compass and entrance direction (for vastu filtering), a furniture toggle, and a mini light strip reusing engine 1.

**Simplify or remove:**
- Draw-on-load animation everywhere: hero only.
- The separate light-study page: absorbed into 1 and 3.
- The "Making The Fold" log: plain content pages, not interactive.
- The honest map: a simple SVG in phase 2.

## 6. 3D decision

**No 3D in version 1.**
- Plan + section is how architects explain position.
- The evidence (report 1) favours plans over tours.
- The heaviest pages observed (Total Environment's auto-embedded tour, the 25 Residences preloader) were the worst experiences.

**One justified candidate for version 2:**
- A line-drawn terrain-plus-massing model of the fold, opened from the locator by tap, on capable devices only.
- Topography is the one thing 2D conveys poorly.
- Build it only if prototype testing shows people misread position or level from the two-view locator.

## 7. Technical approach

- **Unchanged from report 1:** Vite multi-page, Tailwind v4 tokens in `@theme` shared with SVG and Canvas, vanilla ES modules, GSAP (ScrollTrigger/DrawSVG) only for the section, native scroll.
- **Living section:**
  - One inline SVG; the sun position is computed from a small solar-angle function.
  - The shadow wedges are 2D polygons, with no WebGL.
  - It fits a ~150–200 KB JS budget and renders a static noon state without JS.
- **Lessons from what I observed:**
  - No preloaders.
  - Never auto-embed tours or YouTube; use a poster and load on click.
  - Test for horizontal overflow (the Perigon bug).
  - Consent defaults to declined (unlike Chelsea Barracks' pre-ticked banner).
- **Accessibility:**
  - The time control is a native range input with text output ("7:00 am, March — morning sun reaches the living room").
  - The locator uses roving tabindex, with the list as an equal alternative.
  - Targets ≥ 24 px; status is never conveyed by colour alone.
  - WCAG 2.2: 2.5.7 (dragging alternatives) and 2.4.11 (focus not obscured).

## 8. Verified facts vs. open uncertainties

**Verified, via a third-party reproduction of the circular** (ibclaw.in; I did not retrieve the official K-RERA PDF). K-RERA Circular RERA/ADM/CR-4/2019-20, dated 14/11/2019, requires:
- Registration number **top-right** in print, hoarding or other visual ads, lettering **≥ half the project-name font size**.
- `rera.karnataka.gov.in` must be mentioned.
- **No "subject to change" disclaimer.**
- The "RERA Registered" information is ≥ 10% of the ad's length/breadth in print.
- The number "prominently" mentioned in electronic media and SMS.
- The number on the site display board.

**What this means for a fictional project:**
- My understanding, not legal advice: the RERA Act bars advertising an unregistered project.
- So the site must read unmistakably as a portfolio concept and never as an offer:
  - a persistent "Concept project — not an offer, not registered" title-block line
  - a placeholder where the RERA number would be (the top-right title block mirrors the convention without faking a number)
  - prices shown as "illustrative"
  - demo-only forms and WhatsApp

**Preliminary name checks (web search only — NOT legal or trademark clearance):**
- **"The Fold":** a published, award-winning residential project in Dubai (Tkdp, Jumeirah). Same name, same sector, different country. I found no Indian project of that name.
- **"Form & Field":** no real-estate developer of that name was found. A "Form + Field" art-consulting and interiors practice appears in a business directory; its jurisdiction is unclear.
- **Not done:** I did not run an IP India search (it needs interactive search). The earlier note about an "F&F" fashion-label conflict remains unverified.
- Recommendation: keep both names as working names, and decide after a proper IP India and WIPO search if the portfolio goes public.

**Still to verify:**
- mobile and responsive behaviour of every reference;
- West 8, Milieu, Neometro, The OWO and Snøhetta, which I did not see visually;
- typeface licensing;
- the official K-RERA PDF;
- whether a valley-type landform is plausible for typical North Bengaluru parcels; frame it as typology, never as a specific site.
